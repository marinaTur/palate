Built to age, and it shows: firm, structured, one dominant style rather than a wide range. Old World versions (Bordeaux) lean more herbal and savory with a stronger green-pepper streak; New World versions (Napa) taste riper and juicier, with softer edges.

3Secondary / tertiary character

Vanilla, clove, baking spice, cedar from oak aging; with bottle age: tobacco, leather, graphite.

4Commonly confused with

Merlot — Cabernet's tannin feels firmer/more angular and grips the sides of the mouth; Merlot's is rounder, softer, "plush." Secondary: Syrah/Shiraz, told apart by its black pepper note (rotundone) that roughly 1 in 10 people cannot smell at all.

5Food pairing

Complement — its high tannin wants fat (red meat, aged hard cheese) to soften against.

Global prevalence

World's #1 most-planted wine grape variety, ~341,000 hectares, roughly 5% of total world vineyard area (OIV Focus 2017).

Where to find it

Syrah Shiraz

Same grape, two regional names — always shown together per the standing decision, rather than picking one as "correct."

1Primary aroma

Blackberry, black cherry, plum (Syrah); riper blueberry, blackberry jam, cooked plum (Shiraz).

2Structure

Body
Medium-plus to full
Tannin
High, firm, fine-grained

Almost two different wines under one name. Syrah (Northern Rhône, cooler climate) is the elegant side: peppery, floral, restrained. Shiraz (Barossa, warmer climate) is the bold side: jammy, riper, more alcohol.

3Secondary / tertiary character

Black pepper, caused by rotundone, which this grape produces in greater concentrations than almost any other variety. Oak aging (more extensive in Shiraz) adds vanilla, chocolate, sweet spice.

4Commonly confused with

Malbec — a genuinely weaker, more casual-consumer-level pairing than most in this file, flagged explicitly rather than presented at equal confidence.

5Food pairing

Complement — pepper and firm tannin structure classically pair with peppercorn-crusted or char-grilled meats.

Sweetness

Finish

Colour

Deep ruby-purple to opaque, inky black when young — darker even than Cabernet Sauvignon.

Global prevalence

World's #6 most-planted wine grape, ~190,000 hectares.

Where to find it

Ugni Blanc Trebbiano

1Primary aroma

Apple, citrus, lemon, grapefruit, subtle tropical notes, with herbal undertones in cooler-climate examples.

3Secondary / tertiary character

Minimal by design — a deliberately, famously neutral flavor profile, prized specifically because it doesn't compete with what's built on top of it.

Where to find it

Previously this card only linked to Cognac — a real gap, since Trebbiano Toscano is actually Tuscany's most-planted white grape (its historical role blended into Chianti itself, banned by the 1996 DOCG reform). Confirmed via DNA/ampelology sources that this is genuinely the same grape, not the same-named-but-different Trebbiano d'Abruzzo or Trebbiano Romagnolo — see PROJECT_MEMORY.md for the full research trail before assuming this link is settled.

1Primary aroma

Two very different personalities depending on where it's grown: cut grass, green pepper, and gooseberry in cooler spots, or passionfruit, guava, and grapefruit in warmer ones.

2Structure

Body
Light to medium
Tannin
None (white grape)
Acidity
High to very high
ABV
12–14%

Always leans dry and zesty. Loire Valley versions taste more restrained and mineral; Marlborough versions taste louder and more tropical — neither is the "right" one, just two equally valid takes on the same grape.

3Secondary / tertiary character

Usually kept simple on purpose — made in stainless steel so nothing gets in the way of the fresh aroma above. A small minority age it in oak for a rounder, creamier style.

4Commonly confused with

Verdejo — close enough that Spain's Rueda region legally allows the two grapes to be blended together under the same label.

5Food pairing

Contrast — its zing cuts right through goat cheese, oysters, and green vegetables that would clash with a rounder white.
