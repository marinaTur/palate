Cabernet Sauvignon

Black currant, black cherry, blackberry, plum — and a distinctive green bell pepper note, especially in cooler-climate or less-ripe examples.

Built to age, and it shows: firm, structured, one dominant style rather than a wide range. Old World versions (Bordeaux) lean more herbal and savory with a stronger green-pepper streak; New World versions (Napa) taste riper and juicier, with softer edges.

Secondary / tertiary character

Vanilla, clove, baking spice, cedar from oak aging; with bottle age: tobacco, leather, graphite.

Commonly confused with

Merlot — Cabernet's tannin feels firmer and grips the sides of the mouth; Merlot's is rounder and "plush."

Food pairing

Complement — its high tannin wants fat (red meat, aged hard cheese) to soften against.

Finish

Colour

Deep ruby to garnet — one of the deepest reds, from thick skins.

Global prevalence

World's #1 most-planted wine grape, ~341,000 hectares.

Always leans dry and zesty. Loire Valley versions taste more restrained and mineral; Marlborough versions taste louder and more tropical — neither is the "right" one, just two equally valid takes on the same grape.

Secondary / tertiary character

Usually kept simple on purpose — made in stainless steel so nothing gets in the way of the fresh aroma above.

Commonly confused with

Verdejo — close enough that Spain's Rueda region legally allows the two grapes to be blended under the same label.

Food pairing

Contrast — its zing cuts right through goat cheese, oysters, and green vegetables.

SyrahShiraz

Syrah / Shiraz

Blackberry, black cherry, plum (Syrah); riper blueberry, blackberry jam, cooked plum (Shiraz).

Body
Medium+ to full
Tannin
High, firm

Almost two different wines under one name. Syrah (Northern Rhône, cooler climate) is the elegant side: peppery, floral, restrained. Shiraz (Barossa, warmer climate) is the bold side: jammy, riper, more alcohol.

Secondary / tertiary character

Black pepper, from a compound called rotundone — this grape produces more of it than almost any other variety. Oak aging (more in Shiraz) adds vanilla, chocolate, sweet spice.

Commonly confused with

Malbec — a genuinely weaker pairing than most, flagged honestly rather than overstated.

Food pairing

Complement — pepper and firm tannin pair with peppercorn-crusted or char-grilled meats.

Sweetness

Dry

Finish

Long

Colour

Deep ruby-purple to inky black when young.

Global prevalence

World's #6 most-planted wine grape, ~190,000 hectares.

Sparkling grape

Glera

Green apple, pear, white peach, with floral notes like honeysuckle and acacia blossom.

Body
Light–medium
Tannin
None (white)
Acidity
High
ABV
8.5–12.5%

Kept deliberately simple and fresh — made mostly in steel tanks so the fruity, floral character stays front and center rather than picking up bready notes from bottle aging.

Commonly confused with

Not a taste mix-up but a real naming one: the EU renamed this grape from "Prosecco" to "Glera" in 2009 specifically so "Prosecco" could stay a protected place-name, not a grape name anyone could use.

Where to find it

Red grape

Pinot Noir

Red cherry, raspberry, strawberry, cranberry, with floral notes of violet and rose.

Body
Light–medium
Tannin
Low–medium, silky
Acidity
High
ABV
12–14.5%

Delicate rather than powerful, and that's by design — the pleasure here is finesse, not force. Young bottles taste like fresh red fruit; older ones shift toward dried fruit, mushroom, and forest-floor notes.

Commonly confused with

Gamay — a genetic relative sharing the same light, high-acid, low-tannin shape, but more overtly floral and candied where Pinot Noir shows more structure and earthiness.

Open questions for this variant

1. Is red/white/sparkling the right 3-way split for colour-coding, or does fortified (Sherry-style, not in the 27 yet but a real wine category) eventually need its own colour too?

2. The wide tiles for combined-name grapes (Syrah/Shiraz, Ugni Blanc/Trebbiano) break the 2-column grid rhythm on purpose, to signal "this one's different." Does that read clearly, or just look like a layout glitch?

3. Full-screen detail view vs. the previous mockup's inline-expand-in-place — which fits this app's established pattern better? (Regions.jsx today expands inline, never full-screen; this is a real departure worth confirming deliberately, not just because it looks more current.)

4. Red/White/Sparkling classification isn't a real field in grapes.js yet — it was inferred per-grape just for this mockup. If this direction is chosen, adding it as a real schema field is a small but real follow-up task, not automatic.
