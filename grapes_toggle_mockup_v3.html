Built to age, and it shows: firm, structured, one dominant style rather than a wide range. Old World versions (Bordeaux) lean more herbal and savory with a stronger green-pepper streak; New World versions (Napa) taste riper and juicier, with softer edges.

Secondary / tertiary character

Vanilla, clove, baking spice, cedar from oak aging; with bottle age: tobacco, leather, graphite.

Commonly confused with

Merlot — Cabernet's tannin feels firmer and grips the sides of the mouth; Merlot's is rounder and "plush."

Food pairing

Complement — its high tannin wants fat (red meat, aged hard cheese) to soften against.

Delicate rather than powerful, and that's by design — the pleasure here is finesse, not force. Young bottles taste like fresh red fruit; older ones shift toward dried fruit, mushroom, and forest-floor notes.

Commonly confused with

Gamay — a genetic relative sharing the same light, high-acid, low-tannin shape, but more overtly floral and candied where Pinot Noir shows more structure and earthiness.

Food pairing

Complement, unusually flexible for a red — pairs like a food-friendly white would (salmon, mushroom dishes, roast chicken).

Syrah / Shiraz

Blackberry, black cherry, plum (Syrah); riper blueberry, blackberry jam, cooked plum (Shiraz).

Body
Medium+ to full
Tannin
High, firm

Almost two different wines under one name. Syrah (Northern Rhône, cooler climate) is the elegant side: peppery, floral, restrained. Shiraz (Barossa, warmer climate) is the bold side: jammy, riper, more alcohol.

Secondary / tertiary character

Black pepper, from a compound called rotundone — this grape produces more of it than almost any other variety. Oak aging (more in Shiraz) adds vanilla, chocolate, sweet spice.

Commonly confused with

Malbec — a genuinely weaker pairing than most, flagged honestly rather than overstated.

Food pairing

Complement — pepper and firm tannin pair with peppercorn-crusted or char-grilled meats.
